Why it matters

Industrial incidents are operational problems first.

When an industrial controller is attacked, the questions that matter are about the process: is the plant safe, can the operator display be trusted, and what should the team do without making things worse. Those judgements take practice, and most security training never puts a running process in front of the people who will have to make them.

A process that keeps running

Tank levels rise and fall, valves cycle, and safety interlocks act under real control logic. The incident happens while the plant operates, just as it would on shift.

Screens that can mislead

Some attacks change the process. Others change only what the operator sees. Teams learn to cross-check the field before they act on a reading.

Evidence you can query

Industrial network traffic is captured during each run and made searchable. Commands sent from the attacker workspace appear alongside the controller's own traffic, attributed to their source.

Exercise scenarios

Two distinct lessons in industrial incident response.

Each scenario is launched by the instructor against the running plant, then reset to a verified known-good state before the next one begins.

Unauthorized command

The valve that should not open

An attacker forces a tank's discharge valve open while the tank sits in its safe operating band. The team watches the controller's interlock respond, determines whether the process was harmed, and uses the captured control traffic to show that the interlock held.

LessonHow a safety interlock behaves under attack, and how to prove whether a physical consequence occurred.

Manipulation of view

False readings on the operator display

An attacker holds a false near-overflow reading on the operator display while the real tank level is normal. The team must recognize the deception by cross-checking the field, and avoid a disruptive over-reaction to a plant that is running normally.

LessonTreat the HMI as one source of truth, not the only one. Verify before you act.

How it works

One exercise loop, run end to end.

  1. Access the environment

    Participants open the operator HMI, exercise console, and investigation tools in a web browser. The instructor facilitates the session.

  2. Observe the process

    The team learns what normal looks like: operating bands, valve cadence, and the controller's routine traffic.

  3. Work the scenario

    An attack is launched against the running plant. The team observes the symptoms and decides whether the process is actually at risk.

  4. Analyze the evidence

    Operational readings and captured industrial network traffic are examined together to reconstruct what happened and when.

  5. Review the results

    An after-action report summarizes the attack, the process impact, the evidence, and the scoring, and anchors the debrief.
