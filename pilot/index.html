Pilot program

A one-day guided exercise for your team.

Up to five participants act as the security and engineering team for a running water-treatment plant, work two live incidents with an instructor, and leave with a written after-action report.

Format
1 day, guided

Who it is for

Teams that defend, run, or advise on industrial systems.

  • OT and ICS security teams at water, energy, manufacturing, and other industrial operators
  • Controls engineering teams who want IT and OT staff to practice the same incident together
  • Security consultancies and incident response firms building OT capability
  • Training and academic programs developing an applied ICS security track

No prior ICS exercise experience is required. The day works best with a mix of security investigation and process or controls familiarity across the group.

What is included

Everything needed for the day.

  • A prepared exercise environment, verified before the session
  • An instructor who facilitates the scenarios and debriefs
  • Plant orientation and a baseline of normal operations
  • Two live incident scenarios with guided debriefs
  • Evidence investigation in the telemetry dashboard
  • Per-scenario after-action reports and a consolidated team review

The day

A representative schedule.

Roughly six contact hours. Timing is adjusted to your time zone and breaks.

TimeBlockWhat happens
0:00 to 0:30Plant orientationIntroductions, roles, and a tour of the process, the controller, the HMI, and the investigation tools.
0:30 to 1:00BaselineThe team watches the plant run normally and identifies operating bands, valve cadence, and routine control traffic.
1:15 to 2:30Scenario 1Unauthorized command. The team observes, investigates, and works the incident to a recommendation.
2:30 to 3:15Debrief 1What happened at the process, control, and evidence layers, and what the team saw, missed, and concluded.
4:00 to 5:15Scenario 2Manipulation of view. The operator display is deceived while the plant runs normally.
5:15 to 5:45Debrief 2Guided walkthrough of the second incident and the team's decisions.
5:45 to 6:30Team reviewConsolidated after-action review across both scenarios, findings, and next steps.

Learning objectives

What participants practice.

  • Reading a live industrial process well enough to tell normal from abnormal
  • Distinguishing a real process excursion from a manipulated display
  • Investigating captured OT network evidence alongside operational readings
  • Classifying activity using MITRE ATT&CK for ICS techniques
  • Making containment decisions that protect the process, including when to verify before acting
  • Communicating an incident with a defensible timeline

Questions

Pilot questions.

Is the plant real hardware?

No. The process is a physics-based simulation of a two-tank water-treatment plant. The control logic is real compiled PLC code running on an OpenPLC runtime, and the network traffic between controller and process is real Modbus TCP.

Do participants attack the plant themselves?

In the standard pilot, the instructor launches each scenario and participants respond as the defending team. An isolated attacker workspace can be used for guided offensive demonstrations where that suits the group.

Does anything connect to our systems?

No. The exercise environment is self-contained and is never connected to customer networks or industrial systems. Participants only need a web browser.

Is the exercise delivered remotely or on-site?

The delivery format is agreed during planning for each engagement. Either way, the instructor prepares and verifies the environment before the session.

What do we receive afterwards?

The after-action reports for each scenario run and the findings from the consolidated team review.
